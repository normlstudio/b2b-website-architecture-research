A redirect spreadsheet can look complete while sending readers to pages that answer a different question. The status code may be correct, but the information architecture is not.

## Compare page purpose, not only keywords

I would document the subject, audience, decision, evidence, and next action owned by each old URL. Then compare that record with the proposed destination. Similar wording does not always mean equivalent purpose.

When several old pages become one new page, the destination must carry the useful information those pages owned. Otherwise the redirect hides a content loss. When no honest replacement exists, a clear removal may be better than routing every URL to the homepage.

## Update the whole reference system

Redirects are one layer. Internal links, navigation, canonicals, structured data, XML sitemaps, alternate-language references, campaign links, and important external profiles should point to the current destination whenever the team controls them.

The map should also define chains, loops, query handling, trailing-slash rules, and case behavior. Test representative and high-value URLs on the public host after release.

## Preserve the decision record

Keep the old URL, new URL, reason, owner, approval, test result, and review date. That record helps future editors understand whether a redirect represents a renamed page, merged topic, removed offer, or temporary campaign decision.
