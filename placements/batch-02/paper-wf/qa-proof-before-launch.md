A website can feel ready in a review meeting and still fail under a real visitor path. Launch approval becomes more dependable when every important claim and interaction has an observable check.

## Define the critical paths

I would name the journeys that matter most: understand a service, find relevant proof, contact the team, submit a form, schedule a conversation, use the site on a phone, and return through a shared or search result link.

Each path needs an expected outcome and evidence. Screenshots help with layout. Automated checks help with status codes, links, forms, metadata, and responsive constraints. Human review is still needed for meaning, hierarchy, readability, and whether the page answers the buyer’s question.

## Test the operating environment

The public host, domain, analytics, consent behavior, email delivery, integrations, cache, redirects, sitemap, robots rules, and error pages should be checked in the environment that will serve visitors. A passing local build does not prove the production route.

Record the tested URL, viewport, date, result, and reviewer. A failed check needs an owner and another full verification after correction; a silent patch is not evidence.

## Keep claims tied to records

Published work should distinguish documented outcomes, client-reported outcomes, and design or implementation observations. Case studies need the same discipline as launch testing because readers use them to judge fit.
