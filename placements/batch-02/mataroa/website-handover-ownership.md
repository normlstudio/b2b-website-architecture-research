A launch can look finished while the team receiving the website still lacks the knowledge needed to operate it. Credentials alone are not a handover. The real test is whether the new owner can make a routine change, verify it, and recover when something goes wrong.

## Map systems and responsibility

I would begin with a service register covering the domain, DNS, hosting, source repository, deployment, CMS, analytics, search tools, forms, integrations, consent controls, email delivery, and monitoring. Each entry needs an accountable owner, access route, recovery method, and reason the service exists.

Secrets should remain in the approved credential system. The handover document should point to the record rather than repeat a password.

## Teach tasks, not screens

Training is easier to maintain when it is organized around real jobs: update a service, publish an article, replace a team photo, review a form submission, change a redirect, restore a previous release, or report a defect.

Each procedure should identify the source of truth, required approval, verification step, and boundary where an editor should stop and involve a developer. Page-by-page instructions tend to repeat themselves and become stale when shared components change.

## Rehearse one safe release

Before closing the handover, ask the receiving operator to complete a controlled edit. Confirm preview behavior, approval, publication, cache or build status, responsive output, metadata, and rollback. Record questions that expose missing documentation.
