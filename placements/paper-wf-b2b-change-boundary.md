# The change boundary every B2B website handoff should define

A website handoff is incomplete when the next developer, marketer, or content owner cannot tell what they may change safely. The missing artifact is often a change boundary: a short record of which decisions are foundational, which parts are reusable, and which details are intentionally flexible.

## Record the foundation

Start with the choices that shape the whole system:

- audiences, category, offer, proof, objections, and primary actions;
- navigation, page types, buyer routes, and internal-link rules;
- structured content fields, reusable components, and publishing ownership;
- source control, environments, deployment, analytics, consent, search infrastructure, accessibility, and QA.

These are not all immutable. They simply require a deliberate decision and a system-level check before they change.

## Separate safe edits from architectural edits

A safe content edit should not require a developer to reconstruct the website’s reasoning. An architectural edit may change templates, data relationships, URL behavior, measurement, or the sequence in which a buyer encounters the offer and evidence.

Documenting that distinction helps a team avoid two common failures: freezing a website because nobody trusts the system, or changing structural behavior as if it were ordinary page copy.
