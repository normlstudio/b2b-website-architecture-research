# Choose a CMS by the editing model, not the demo screen

A page builder or CMS can feel easy during a demonstration and become difficult
when several people must maintain shared facts, reusable sections, approvals,
and technical controls.

## List the real editing jobs

Record who updates services, people, work, resources, metadata, redirects,
navigation, forms, and structured data. For each job, define the source of
truth, required approval, preview behavior, publication state, and rollback.

Then identify which decisions belong to structured fields, which need flexible
editorial content, and which should stay in code. Giving every editor control
over every visual setting can create design drift and accidental technical
changes. Locking everything in code can make routine publishing unnecessarily
dependent on developers.

## Price the ownership boundary

The platform decision includes migrations, plugin or dependency updates,
performance, accessibility, security, testing, documentation, and future
redesigns. The right model is the one the actual team can operate safely.

Norml documented
[why it stopped recommending page builders as a default](https://norml.studio/blog/why-we-stopped-recommending-page-builders),
framing the choice around structured content, maintainability, and control
rather than a universal ban.

Published by Norml Studio and prepared by Max Tymoshyn, Founder, CEO, and
Architect at Norml Studio. Different teams may reach a different platform
decision after applying the same ownership questions.
